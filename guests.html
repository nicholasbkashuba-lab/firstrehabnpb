Episode 18

Elyse Marrone, RD/LDN CGP

Registered dietitian, functional and integrative nutrition · The Lifestyle Nutrition Institute · Jupiter, FL

On the show

“I want to be healthy, healthy, healthy, dead.”

Elyse described herself as a functionally and integratively trained dietitian who looks at all seven pillars of a patient’s lifestyle: food first, exercise, sleep, stress management, supplementation, hydration and community. She calls the goal the triple HD plan. After a breast cancer diagnosis, she said, the first place she looks is the gut, where she puts 80 to 90% of the immune system. She closed on sleep, recommending blue light blocker glasses from 8 o’clock in the evening to help the body start producing melatonin.

Background

  • She is a licensed and registered dietitian/nutritionist and a certified gluten-free practitioner (CGP).
  • She completed her undergraduate degree at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, and her clinical internship and graduate training at UMass Medical Center in Worcester through Framingham State University.
  • She has trained in functional medicine and nutritional biochemistry through the Institute for Functional Medicine (IFM) and the American Academy for Anti-Aging Medicine (A4M).
  • She is a member of the Dietitians in Functional Medicine practice group.
  • She lectures on nutrition topics at universities, medical offices and other venues for practitioners, students, patients and the public.
  • Her practice’s site says she has more than twenty years of experience in the field of nutrition.

Episode 18

Danielle Armstrong

Owner of Pilates Life and Pilates master instructor · Pilates Life · Palm Beach Gardens, FL

On the show

“Prevention is better than a cure.”

Danielle owns Pilates Life on Northlake and is a former dancer. She said that this year in particular she has worked with many women in their mid 60s to mid 70s through a breast cancer diagnosis and treatment, keeping them active and moving. She traced her training back to Joseph Pilates through Romana Kryzanowska and Lolita San Miguel, and told the story of Eve Gentry, the dancer Pilates helped regain her full range of motion after a radical mastectomy.

Background

  • She grew up in Western New York and is an honors graduate of SUNY Purchase College, where she earned a BFA from the Conservatory of Dance and was first introduced to the Pilates method.
  • In 2002 she completed more than 600 hours of teacher training and was certified through Pilates Inc. under the direction of Romana Kryzanowska, along with Ton Voogt and Michael Fritzke.
  • She opened her first studio in Norwalk, Connecticut, in 2005 and moved to Florida in 2009, where she has taught dance and Pilates, including at the Harid Conservatory.
  • She met Lolita San Miguel in 2012, completed Lolita’s Pilates Master Mentor Program in 2014, and was selected to teach Lolita’s Legacy, Lolita’s teacher training program.
  • Her studio opened in 2011 as Classical Pilates at The Florida School for Dance Education and, renamed Pilates Life, is now home to Lolita’s Pilates Center.

Episode 17

Dr. Donald Goodwin, MD

Radiologist and co-founder of Palm Beach Radiology, now TGH Imaging · TGH Imaging · North Palm Beach, FL

On the show

“Our job is to find it as small and early as possible, because the smaller the lesion, the earlier the stage, the better the prognosis.”

Dr. Don Goodwin, a radiologist Dave says has been reading images for more than 50 years, opened Breast Cancer Awareness Month by walking through when screening should start and how often. He sides with annual mammograms from age 40, explains why dense breast tissue can hide small cancers and how ultrasound and MRI add detection, and puts the radiation from a 3D mammogram at about 0.4 millisieverts, roughly one flight across the country. He also reminded listeners that men get breast cancer too.

Background

  • He is a radiologist and co-founder of Palm Beach Radiology in North Palm Beach, which opened in 2007.
  • Tampa General Hospital acquired Palm Beach Radiology in October 2022, and the center became TGH Imaging at the same North Palm Beach location.
  • When the practice joined Tampa General, he was one of the three physicians named to continue leading it.
  • The North Palm Beach center is accredited by the American College of Radiology and offers 3D mammography, breast MRI, ultrasound and image-guided biopsies.

Episode 16

Dr. Matthew Butler, MD

Hand and upper extremity orthopedic surgeon · HSS Florida · West Palm Beach, FL

On the show

“That’s how we all interact with our environment… something that people take for granted until it starts bothering them.”

Dr. Butler, who in his words takes care of people’s arms, mostly the elbow, wrist and hand, explained why tennis elbow is so common in middle age even among people who never play tennis, and how he adds a collagen patch for the stubborn cases. He described the elbow injuries now showing up in young pitchers who play one sport year round, and a newly FDA cleared thumb joint replacement for basal thumb arthritis, built like a dual mobility hip, as an alternative to removing the trapezium bone.

Background

  • He is a board certified hand and upper extremity surgeon at HSS Florida, treating conditions of the hand, wrist, elbow and shoulder.
  • He graduated magna cum laude in biochemistry from the University of Notre Dame and earned his medical degree at the University of Florida, where he was inducted into the Alpha Omega Alpha honor society.
  • He completed his orthopedic surgery residency at Vanderbilt University and a fellowship in hand and microvascular surgery at the Indiana Hand to Shoulder Center.
  • Before returning to Florida, he spent more than a decade in practice at a large upper extremity center in the Midwest.
  • He is board certified by the American Board of Orthopedic Surgery with a certificate of added qualification in hand surgery.

Episode 15

Susan Mann

Founder and president, Bright Minds Processing · Bright Minds Processing · Palm Beach Gardens, FL

On the show

“They can’t be gifted if they can’t do their schoolwork.”

Susan Mann told Dave and Mike how teachers called her two children gifted while every test put them near the bottom, until an audiologist identified auditory and visual processing disorders. A retired San Francisco police officer, not a clinician, she now runs Bright Minds Processing so other families get answers sooner. She described the therapy as 16 weeks, one session a week with exercises five evenings a week at home, starting with a $30 screening, and the basketball program she is building in Palm Beach Gardens with former NBA player Dwayne McClain.

Background

  • She is the founder and president of Bright Minds Processing, where she oversees the strategic direction and operations of the nonprofit.
  • Bright Minds Processing Inc. is a Florida 501(c)(3) nonprofit that works to raise awareness of auditory and visual processing disorders and to bring solutions into schools.
  • She started the organization after both of her children were diagnosed with auditory and visual processing disorders, a journey she tells in her own words on its site.
  • The organization runs the McClain Basketball Academy, led by former professional player Dwayne “D-Train” McClain, where students must keep their grades up to take part.
  • In April 2026 she appeared on CBS12’s ARC Florida with Dwayne McClain ahead of the group’s first fundraising event at the SoFi Center in Palm Beach Gardens.

Episode 14

Dr. Chaim Arlosoroff, MD

Orthopedic trauma surgeon, St. Mary’s Medical Center · Orthopedic Care Specialists · North Palm Beach, FL

On the show

“Going 15 versus 50 is the difference between a scrape and a cracked skull.”

Dr. Arlosoroff, on staff at St. Mary’s Medical Center for 30 years, described the rise in e-bike injuries he sees in its Level 1 trauma center and ER. He puts Palm Beach County’s increase at 130% from 2023 to 2024, says about 83% of injured riders were not wearing helmets and the average age is 13, and explains how modified governors let bikes built for 15 to 20 mph reach 50 or 60. He also told how his path to medicine began at 16, volunteering in an operating room during the 1973 Yom Kippur War.

Background

  • He is an orthopedic surgeon with Orthopedic Care Specialists in North Palm Beach.
  • His hospital affiliations are St. Mary’s Medical Center and Palm Beach Gardens Medical Center.
  • His medical training is listed at Hahnemann University Hospital.

Episode 12

Dr. Michael Leighton, MD

Orthopedic surgeon, sports medicine and joint replacement · Palm Beach Orthopaedic Institute · Palm Beach Gardens, FL

On the show

“This is the best operation that’s done in orthopedics… the single best operation that Medicare pays for.”

Dr. Leighton, whom Dave has referred patients to since 1994, broke down total hip replacement, including the anterior and posterior approaches and why he steers heavier or older patients toward the posterior approach. He puts lifetime infection risk at under 1 to 2 percent, higher in smokers, nicotine vapers and diabetics with an A1C above 7.5, and explained why dental hygiene matters before joint surgery and why bone needs about six weeks to grow into a modern non cemented implant. He also noted that most of the old dislocation precautions are gone.

Background

  • He specializes in sports medicine and arthroscopic surgery of the knee and shoulder, as well as total joint replacement of the knee, hip and shoulder, and has practiced in Palm Beach County since 1994.
  • He is board certified by the American Board of Orthopaedic Surgery and holds its subspecialty certification in Orthopaedic Sports Medicine.
  • He earned a Bachelor of Arts in philosophy at Duke University and his medical degree at Robert Wood Johnson Medical School (Rutgers), where he also completed his residency.
  • He completed a sports medicine fellowship at Rush St. Luke’s Medical Center in Chicago in 1994.
  • He performs both posterior and direct anterior total hip replacement using minimally invasive techniques, and robotic assisted (Mako) total and partial knee replacement.
  • He serves as team physician for local high schools and sits on the Board of Governors of Jupiter Medical Center.

Episode 11

Paul Joyce

Runs New Life HRT, peptide and hormone replacement training · New Life HRT

On the show

“You definitely have to make sure you’re getting enough protein.”

Paul Joyce, a friend of Dave’s for 22 years, explained what a peptide is, a short chain of amino acids, usually under 50, and where the GLP-1 medicines came from, a gut hormone the body already makes. He was candid about the muscle that weight loss can take with it and used himself as the example: he says he went from 209 pounds to 153 and took it too far. He also described having six research only peptide websites tested, with all six coming back with major flaws.

Episode 10

Dr. Zachary McVicker, MD

Sports medicine orthopedic surgeon, hip arthroscopy and preservation · Paley Orthopedic & Spine Institute · West Palm Beach and Jupiter, FL

On the show

“Instead of being round, it’s more like an oval peg in a round hole.”

Dr. McVicker explained femoroacetabular impingement, the hip problem that often shows up as back or groin pain. He described the two forms, the cam lesion where the ball is misshapen and the pincer lesion where the socket covers too much of it, and said pincer lesions are the harder of the two to treat. He compared the labrum to a gasket that keeps a suction seal and spreads weight across the joint, and said that arthroscopic surgery is minimally invasive and keeps your own hip, along with the balance and proprioception a replacement cannot give back.

Background

  • He earned his medical degree at the University of Toledo College of Medicine and Life Sciences and completed his orthopedic surgery training at the University of Rochester Medical Center.
  • He completed a sports medicine fellowship at the Cedars-Sinai Kerlan-Jobe Institute in Los Angeles, serving as an assistant team physician for teams including the Los Angeles Rams, Los Angeles Dodgers and USC football.
  • Since moving to Jupiter he has served as an assistant team physician for the Miami Marlins and the Jupiter Hammerheads and as team physician for Jupiter Christian High School.
  • He has a special interest in hip injuries, including femoroacetabular impingement, labral tears, hip dysplasia, and gluteus medius and hamstring injuries, and is trained in hip arthroscopy and cartilage restoration.
  • He sees patients at the Paley Institute offices in West Palm Beach and Jupiter.

Episode 9

Dr. Vani Sabesan, MD

Orthopaedic shoulder and elbow surgeon · Orthopedic Center of Palm Beach County · Atlantis, FL

On the show

“We’re really good at fixing them. We’re not great at getting them to heal.”

Dr. Sabesan talked frankly about the limits of surgery. She puts rotator cuff problems at roughly 17 million people and about one in three people over 60, and said surgeons are good at repairing cuffs but not yet good at getting them to heal. She described a head to head study in which patients who started moving within a week did as well as those kept in a sling for six weeks, and a survey in which 98 percent of shoulder replacement patients said their therapist had a big impact on the outcome. Mike noted she is the first woman chosen to lead the Florida Orthopaedic Society.

Background

  • She is a board certified orthopaedic surgeon and shoulder and elbow specialist with the Orthopedic Center of Palm Beach County.
  • She earned her undergraduate degree at Duke University and her medical degree at Indiana University School of Medicine, then completed her orthopaedic surgery residency at Duke University.
  • She completed fellowship training in shoulder and elbow surgery and sports medicine at the Cleveland Clinic, along with a Doris Duke Clinical Research Fellowship at the University of Iowa.
  • She previously served as Chief of Quality and held the Lang Family Endowed Chair in Orthopaedic Research at Cleveland Clinic Florida, and was program director of the orthopaedic residency at HCA Florida JFK/University of Miami.
  • She has published more than 105 peer reviewed articles and has served on the Florida Orthopaedic Society Board and as its program chair.
  • Outside the operating room she has run more than 25 marathons and completed an Ironman.

Episode 8

Dr. Ryan W. Simovitch, MD

Shoulder surgeon, Director of the Shoulder Division at HSS Florida · HSS Florida · West Palm Beach, FL

On the show

“The ball is on the socket side, the socket is on the ball side.”

Dr. Simovitch explained reverse total shoulder replacement in plain terms. When the rotator cuff is no longer intact, a standard ball and socket replacement lacks stability and can fail early, so the reverse design swaps the two sides to give the shoulder the stability the cuff no longer can. He described the “constant warrior,” the patient who plays pickleball most days of the week with no moderation and no preparation until something gives, and traced his path into orthopedics to an undergraduate anatomy elective at Duke where students dissected a human leg.

Background

  • He is Director of the Shoulder Division and Co-Medical Director at HSS Florida, where he specializes in complex arthroscopic and open shoulder surgery, including shoulder replacement and revision shoulder replacement.
  • He attended Duke University as an undergraduate, earned his medical degree at Chicago Medical School in 2000, and completed his orthopedic residency at Duke University Medical Center, where he provided team coverage for the Duke men’s basketball and football teams.
  • He completed the Harvard Intercontinental Shoulder Fellowship, training on the Harvard Shoulder Service at Massachusetts General Hospital and at Uniklinik Balgrist in Zurich, Switzerland, where he began working with reverse shoulder replacement before it was widely available in the United States.
  • He is board certified by the American Board of Orthopaedic Surgery and has been practicing since 2006.
  • He serves as a team physician for the Palm Beach Cardinals and the Gulf Coast League Cardinals, St. Louis Cardinals minor league affiliates, and assists with Cardinals players at the Jupiter training and rehab facility.

Episode 7

Dr. Rami J. Elkhechen, MD

Sports medicine orthopedic surgeon · NYU Langone Medical Associates, Palm Beach Gardens · Palm Beach Gardens, FL

On the show

“Cartilage loves range of motion.”

Dr. Elkhechen talked with Dave and Mike about what separates a surgical candidate from someone better served by conservative care, and why he tries non-operative treatment as often as he can first. He described cartilage as “the gold in a vault at a bank,” with the ligaments and meniscus as its security system, and walked through cartilage restoration and the rehab after it. He also does prehab before surgery, especially for ACL patients, because a stronger base going in means a better recovery coming out.

Background

  • He is a Clinical Assistant Professor in the Department of Orthopedic Surgery at NYU Grossman School of Medicine and sees patients at NYU Langone Medical Associates in Palm Beach Gardens.
  • He earned his medical degree at New York University and completed his orthopedic surgery residency at SUNY Downstate in Brooklyn.
  • He completed a sports medicine fellowship at the Ochsner Clinic Foundation in New Orleans.
  • During his fellowship he served as an assistant team physician for the New Orleans Saints and New Orleans Pelicans.
  • He is board certified by the American Board of Orthopaedic Surgery.
  • His practice covers sports medicine and arthroscopic surgery of the shoulder, elbow, hip, knee and ankle, including cartilage procedures and ACL reconstruction.

Episode 6

Dr. Richard L. Weiner, M.D.

Orthopaedic surgeon, hip and knee replacement · Orthopaedic Care Specialists · North Palm Beach, FL

On the show

“You don’t treat an X-ray, you treat the patient.”

Dr. Weiner, who has worked alongside Dave since 1990, spent much of the hour on how to keep people out of the operating room. He described a ladder of care that starts with conservative treatment and physical therapy and treats surgery as the last resort. On knee replacement, he puts it at over 40% of total knee patients who could have had a partial instead, against about 10% in the United States who actually get one, and he explained why a partial can feel more like a normal knee.

Background

  • He earned a Bachelor of Science in chemical engineering from the University of Pennsylvania School of Engineering and Applied Science, then his medical degree from the University of Pennsylvania School of Medicine.
  • He completed his orthopaedic surgery residency at New Jersey Medical School, University of Medicine and Dentistry of New Jersey, in 1991.
  • He is a Diplomate of the American Board of Orthopaedic Surgery and a Fellow of the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons.
  • At St. Mary’s Medical Center in West Palm Beach he served as Chief of Orthopedic Trauma Surgery from 1993 to 2019.
  • His practice profile notes that he uses the Oxford partial knee replacement and dual mobility total hip replacements.
