# Review scripts

Exact wording for the front desk. Copy these rather than improvising, because the improvised
version is where gating language and HIPAA slips creep in. Rules and reasoning live in
`README.md` in this folder.

Style matches the rest of the clinic's outward copy: zero dashes in prose, only the phone number
keeps its own.

---

## 1. At the desk, final visit

Hand the card. One sentence, said the same way to every patient regardless of how the course of
care went.

> "You are all set. If you have a minute this week, a short Google review really helps other
> people in the area find us. The code on this card takes you straight there."

If they ask what to write:

> "Whatever is honest. A sentence is plenty."

**Do not say** any version of "if you were happy with your care", "if we earned five stars", or
"only if you had a good experience". That is gating, out loud. The ask is the same for everyone.

If the patient raises a problem in that moment, drop the review ask entirely and hand it to the
office manager. Fix the thing first. Do not re-ask later as a trade.

---

## 2. Same evening text

Only to patients who gave a mobile and agreed to be contacted by text.

> Thanks for finishing up with us today. If you have a minute, a quick Google review helps
> neighbours find care they can trust: firstrehabnpb.com/review
>
> First Rehabilitation, 561-624-4263

Notes:
- No name in the message. A text that opens "Hi Carol, thanks for your shoulder therapy" is a
  disclosure sitting in a phone that may not be hers alone.
- No mention of condition, therapist or appointment.
- One link, no tracking parameters. Google does not need them and they make the link look junk.

## 3. Five day reminder

Send once, only if no review appeared. Then stop for good.

> Following up once in case it slipped by. If you have a moment, our Google review link is
> firstrehabnpb.com/review and it genuinely helps.
>
> First Rehabilitation, 561-624-4263

## 4. Email version

For patients who prefer email or gave no mobile.

**Subject:** A quick favour from First Rehabilitation

> Thank you for letting us be part of your recovery.
>
> If you have a minute, would you leave us a short Google review? It is the single most useful
> thing a patient can do for us. It helps people nearby decide where to go, and it takes about
> thirty seconds.
>
> firstrehabnpb.com/review
>
> If anything about your experience fell short, we would rather hear it directly. Call the front
> desk at 561-624-4263 and ask for the office manager.
>
> Thank you,
> The team at First Rehabilitation
> 733 US Highway 1, Suite 2A, North Palm Beach, FL 33408

That last paragraph is not gating. It offers a direct line **in addition to** the public link,
which everyone receives. Gating would be routing unhappy patients away from the link instead of
giving them both. Keep the link above the offer to call, and keep it in every send.

## 5. Responding to reviews

Read `README.md` rule 3 before editing any of these. The bar: a stranger reading the reply
should not learn whether this person was ever a patient here, or what for.

### Positive, no detail needed

> Thank you for taking the time to write this. It means a great deal to our team. We are glad
> the experience was a good one.

### Positive, mentions a staff member by name

Thanking the named person is safe. Confirming they treated the reviewer is not.

> Thank you for the kind words. We will make sure Laura hears them. Our team appreciates it more
> than you know.

Never extend it to "Laura always does great work with rotator cuff patients like you."

### Four star, mild criticism

> Thank you for the honest feedback, and for the four stars. We would like to hear more about
> what would have made it a five. Please call our front desk at 561-624-4263 and ask for the
> office manager.

### Negative

Bland on purpose. Do not defend, do not explain, do not correct facts, do not confirm the person
came here.

> We are sorry to hear this. We take feedback like this seriously and would like to understand
> what happened. Please call our front desk at 561-624-4263 and ask for the office manager so we
> can look into it properly.

### Negative and factually wrong

Same reply as above. Word for word. The temptation to say "actually you were never scheduled
with us" is exactly the disclosure to avoid, and it reads badly to every future reader besides.

If a review breaks Google's own content policy, the route is Google's removal flow from the
profile, not a public rebuttal.

---

## What never appears in any of these

- A name the reviewer did not publish themselves
- A condition, body part, procedure, or treatment
- A therapist tied to a specific patient
- An appointment date, a visit count, or a referral source
- Any offer, discount, or entry into anything
- Any phrasing that sorts patients by how happy they are before deciding whether to ask
