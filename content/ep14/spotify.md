The average age of an e bike injury is 13.

Dr. Chaim Arlosoroff has done orthopedic trauma at St. Mary's, a level one
trauma center, for 30 years, and he pulled the South Florida numbers himself.
Palm Beach County up 130% from 2023 to 2024. Broward up 180%. Miami Dade,
including Nicklaus Children's, over 500%. Nationally, e bike injuries reaching
the emergency room are up more than 400% since 2017. In 83% of them no helmet
was worn, and South Florida has seen 16 fatalities in the last four or five
years.

The mechanism is not complicated. A kid buys an electric bike, removes the
governor that caps it at 15 or 20 miles an hour, and rides it at 50 or 60 with
nothing between his head and the concrete. Going 15 versus 50, as Mike puts it,
is the difference between a scrape and a cracked skull.

Chaim is not against the bikes. He rides them himself in Sedona, Park City and
Italy, and makes the case that pedal assist and a modified motor are two
different machines. Dave makes the other case, with numbers: only 20% of
American kids get an hour of exercise a day and 21% are morbidly obese, against
more than 80% and under 5% in the early 1970s.

Before any of the medicine there was tennis. Israel Junior Davis Cup, a world
ranking of 283, a full four year scholarship to Duke sight unseen, and a place
in the Duke Tennis Hall of Fame. The operating room came first though, at 16,
during the 1973 Yom Kippur War, when his mother went back to nursing and
brought him along to clean up after surgery.

Also in this one: what a trauma surgeon sees in a county with dirt bikes to the
west and the ocean to the east, when a fracture does not need surgery at all,
and a joke from Dave that Mike bought completely.
