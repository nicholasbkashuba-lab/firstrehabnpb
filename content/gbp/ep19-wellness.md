keyword: what to do after you finish physical therapy so you do not lose progress (Jupiter)

The day physical therapy ends is usually the day the structure goes away.

Every patient leaves with a written home exercise program, and that is the first step. The next one, for a lot of people, is finding time. Shannon Cake, who is up at 4:30 for a Newsmax show at 9, told our Pain 2 Power listeners her rule: if you are going to get your workout in, do it first thing in the morning, because it is the hour that gets interrupted least.

Mike McGann's version was shorter. It is a matter of priority, not time.

If you want more structure than a folder of exercises, our wellness program offers personal training and small classes with a team that knows your rehab history. Patients drive in from Jupiter for exactly that.

keyword: is it safe to go back to the gym after a joint replacement (Tequesta)

Going back to the gym after a joint replacement is a reasonable goal. The question is how, and who is watching your form when you do.

The months after discharge from post surgical rehabilitation are when people are most likely to lose the range of motion they worked hard for. A supervised program built by the team that treated you picks up from your chart rather than a blank page: which movements are still off the table, when to add weight, and what kind of pain means stop.

At First Rehabilitation, the wellness gym sits inside the clinic, so if something flares up, the therapists who can reassess it are down the hall. That is a big part of why patients from Tequesta keep training with us after therapy ends.

A training session is never a substitute for an evaluation. If something new hurts or will not settle, get it checked.
