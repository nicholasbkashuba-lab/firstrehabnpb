After a stroke, the hard part is often the morning you try to button a shirt with a hand that will not cooperate.

Post stroke occupational therapy in North Palm Beach deals with your actual day. The muscle in that arm is usually intact. The instruction is not arriving, or it arrives scrambled, so the hand closes when you wanted it to open. That is why the work is built around repeated, task specific practice rather than strengthening alone.

What an evaluation sorts out:

• Which daily tasks have become unsafe or impossible
• Whether attention and memory are part of the picture
• How much of the arm and hand function can be retrained

Treatment usually begins the same day as the evaluation, and it is one on one.

keyword: cognitive rehabilitation after injury

This is the piece families are least prepared for.

The arm is visible. The attention span is not. So somebody can look recovered after a stroke or a brain injury and still be unable to follow a three step instruction, hold a conversation, or come back to a task after an interruption. They are usually embarrassed by it, which means nobody hears about it until it causes a problem.

Cognitive rehabilitation after injury works on memory, attention and problem solving, and it is practical rather than abstract. The targets are real: the medication schedule, the grocery list, the route home, the order you do things in to get out the door.

keyword: return to work program after injury

A clearance date on a calendar is not a plan.

A return to work program after injury is graded. It builds toward the real demands of the job through task simulation and conditioning, instead of handing out a blanket clearance at a fixed number of weeks and hoping the job cooperates.

For desk work that usually means a workstation assessment and the adaptive equipment that makes a task repeatable without cost. For anything physical it means loading the actual movements the job requires, in order, until they hold up for a full shift.

The hand is where occupational therapy and certified hand therapy meet.

When a stroke leaves a hand weak, clumsy or partly closed, the upper extremity work runs in coordination between the two programs. Fine motor training, functional use practice, and splinting when the hand needs a position held while it recovers. Laura Drumm, CHT, leads the certified hand therapy side.

That coordination matters more than it sounds, because plenty of patients arrive with something else in the chart already. A shoulder problem, an old wrist fracture, a surgery still healing. The plan has to account for all of it rather than treating the neurological piece in isolation.

keyword: home and workstation setup after a hand or arm injury

Sometimes the fix is the setup, not the exercise.

Home and workstation setup after a hand or arm injury is a real part of occupational therapy, and it is the part that keeps a recovery from sliding backward. A chair at the wrong height, a keyboard that forces the wrist into extension for eight hours, a kitchen where everything you use daily sits on the top shelf. Each one quietly undoes the work you did in the clinic that morning.

An assessment looks at what you actually do and where you do it, then recommends the equipment and the changes that make those tasks safe to repeat. It covers the desk, but it also covers the kitchen, the bathroom and the car door.
