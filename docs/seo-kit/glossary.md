# Physical & Occupational Therapy Glossary: Plain-English Terms for Patients

*Page title (H1). Suggested URL: /resources/therapy-glossary/. Suggested meta description: "A plain-English A-Z glossary of physical therapy, occupational therapy, and insurance terms from First Rehabilitation of North Palm Beach."*

Ever sit in a waiting room and wonder what half the paperwork means? You're not alone. This glossary explains the words you'll hear at First Rehabilitation of North Palm Beach, from "plan of care" to "prior authorization," in everyday language.

Not sure how a term applies to you? Your therapist can walk you through it at your visit.

## B

**Balance training**
Exercises that challenge your steadiness so you're less likely to fall. *Example:* standing on one foot while reaching, or walking heel-to-toe along a line.

## C

**Certified hand therapist (CHT)**
A physical or occupational therapist who has passed a national certification exam in hand and upper-extremity rehab after years of hands-on experience. *Example:* the therapist who guides your recovery after a wrist fracture or tendon repair.

**Coinsurance**
Your share of a covered visit's cost, shown as a percentage, after you've met your deductible. *Example:* with 20% coinsurance, you pay 20% of the allowed amount and your plan pays the rest.

**Copay**
A flat fee you pay at each visit, set by your plan. *Example:* a $30 copay each time you come in.

**CORF (Comprehensive Outpatient Rehabilitation Facility)**
A Medicare-certified facility that provides coordinated outpatient rehabilitation services under physician oversight. *Example:* a clinic that offers physical therapy, occupational therapy, and other rehab services for Medicare patients in one place.

## D

**Deductible**
The amount you pay for covered care each year before your plan starts paying its share. *Example:* if your deductible is $1,000, you pay the first $1,000 of allowed charges.

**Direct access**
Seeing a physical therapist without first getting a doctor's referral. Rules vary by state and by insurance plan, so call us before booking. *Example:* a patient with a sore shoulder calls to schedule an evaluation on their own.

**Discharge**
The end of formal therapy, when you've met your goals or your progress has leveled off. *Example:* you finish therapy with a written home program to keep your gains.

## E

**Electrical stimulation**
A gentle electrical current used to calm pain or help muscles activate, usually alongside exercise. *Example:* small pads placed near a sore knee while you rest.

**Explanation of benefits (EOB)**
A statement from your insurance company showing what was billed, what it paid, and what you owe. It isn't a bill. *Example:* the paper or online summary you get after a visit.

## F

**Fall-risk screening**
Quick tests of strength, balance, and walking that show how likely you are to fall. *Example:* standing up from a chair and walking a short distance while a therapist times you.

**Functional capacity evaluation**

## G

**Gait training**
Practice walking safely and smoothly after an injury, surgery, or illness. *Example:* relearning a steady step pattern after a hip replacement.

## H

**Home exercise program**
A short set of exercises your therapist teaches you to do on your own between visits. *Example:* three exercises, ten minutes a day, with pictures to follow.

## I

**In-network and out-of-network**
In-network providers have a contract with your insurance and usually cost you less. Out-of-network providers don't. *Example:* checking whether a clinic is in-network before your first visit.

**Initial evaluation**
Your first visit. The therapist reviews your history, tests your movement, strength, and daily function, and sets goals with you. *Example:* measuring how far you can bend a sore knee and how well you climb stairs.

## K

**KX modifier (Medicare therapy threshold)**
A billing code that tells Medicare therapy is still medically necessary once a patient's yearly therapy spending passes a set amount. *Example:* your therapist documents why more visits are needed, and the clinic adds the code to your claim.

## L

**Letter of protection (LOP)**
A written agreement, used in some injury cases, where a provider treats you and is paid from a settlement or judgment later. It is not insurance, and your attorney arranges it. *Example:* a car accident patient treated while their attorney handles the claim.

## M

**Manual therapy**
Hands-on techniques that move joints and soft tissue to reduce stiffness and pain. *Example:* a therapist gently mobilizing a stiff shoulder.

**Medicare Advantage**
A private plan that covers Medicare benefits and may have its own rules, copays, and approvals. *Example:* a Humana or Aetna Medicare plan. Check with the plan about authorization.

**Modalities**
Supportive treatments like heat, cold, or electrical stimulation, usually paired with exercise. *Example:* a warm pack before stretching.

## N

**Neuromuscular re-education**
Exercises that retrain coordination, balance, and movement patterns after an injury or neurological problem. *Example:* practicing controlled movements after a stroke.

## O

**Occupational therapy (OT)**
Therapy focused on everyday function, such as dressing, cooking, working, and using your hands and arms. *Example:* relearning to button a shirt after a hand injury.

**Out-of-pocket maximum**
The most you'll pay in a year for covered care before your plan pays 100%. *Example:* once you hit the limit, covered visits cost you nothing more that year.

**Outpatient therapy**
Therapy where you come in for a visit and go home the same day. *Example:* three visits a week for six weeks.

## P

**Personal injury protection (PIP)**
Florida no-fault auto coverage that helps pay medical bills after a crash regardless of who caused it, up to your policy limit. *Example:* using PIP for therapy after a car accident.

**Physical therapy (PT)**
Treatment that restores movement, strength, balance, and function, and helps reduce pain. *Example:* a program of exercises and hands-on care after knee surgery.

**Physician order**
A doctor's written request for therapy. Some plans, and Medicare, require one. *Example:* your surgeon sends an order for six weeks of rehab.

**Plan of care**
A written roadmap of your goals, treatments, how often you'll come, and how long therapy is expected to last. For Medicare, a physician must certify it. *Example:* three visits a week for four weeks, with the goal of climbing stairs without a rail.

**Post-surgical rehabilitation**
Therapy after an operation to restore motion and strength safely. *Example:* rehab after a shoulder or knee procedure, following your surgeon's protocol.

**Prior authorization**
Approval your insurer may require before it will cover therapy. *Example:* we submit the request and records, and the plan approves a set number of visits.

**Progress report**
A written update to your doctor and insurer showing how you're doing against your goals. *Example:* a summary after ten visits.

## R

**Range of motion**
How far a joint can move. *Example:* how high you can lift your arm overhead.

**Re-evaluation**
A mid-course checkup comparing your progress to your goals and adjusting the plan. *Example:* retesting strength and balance after a month of therapy.

## S

**Self-pay**
Paying for visits yourself instead of billing insurance. *Example:* a wellness session paid at checkout. *[TODO: link to current self-pay rates.]*

**Splint (orthosis)**
A custom or ready-made support that protects or positions a hand, wrist, or finger while it heals. *Example:* a thumb splint after a tendon injury, if your therapist recommends one.

## T

**Therapeutic activities**
Real-life tasks used as exercise. *Example:* lifting a box, getting out of a chair, or reaching into a cabinet.

**Therapeutic exercise**
Targeted movements that build strength, flexibility, and endurance. *Example:* leg lifts, band work, or stretching routines.

## W

**Workers' compensation**
Insurance that covers medical care for injuries that happen at work. *Example:* therapy after a job-related shoulder injury, arranged through your employer's carrier.
