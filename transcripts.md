# Episode 10 clip transcripts — Dr. Zach McVicker

Guest: **Dr. Zach McVicker, MD** — orthopedic surgeon, Paley Orthopedic and Spine
Institute, Jupiter and West Palm Beach. Hosts: Dr. Dave Kashuba and Mike McGann.

## 01-oval-peg.mp4

There's a pincer lesion, which is the cup, the acetabulum, covering too much of the ball. And then you have the cam lesion, which is the ball, shaped, misshapen. So I like to tell people, as a visual, instead of being round, it's more like an oval peg in a round hole. So there's a mismatch there.

## 02-gasket.mp4

The labrum, think of it like a gasket. If you have two pipes, you're thinking of a gasket that's creating a seal. For the hip, it creates negative pressure so that when you walk, it spreads the contact pressure, the weight, throughout the whole dome. And if you lose that suction seal, you start increasing pressure on the cartilage in there.

## 03-arthritis-100.mp4

There's something called hip dysplasia, the second most common thing I see. That's the exact opposite of a pincer lesion. Instead of over coverage of the ball, the cup is underdeveloped, so you're point loading on the cartilage. That ends up in arthritis 100 percent of the time, because it's edge loading.

## 04-proprioception.mp4

It's arthroscopic, so little cameras, little poke holes. With the total hip, you're getting back feeling pretty normal pretty quickly. But it's not your own hip. You don't have the proprioception, the balance and stuff, that you would have with your native hip.

## 05-belt-buckle.mp4

Especially in males, I tell people, when you're wearing a belt, the positioning of the pelvis is key. If your belt buckle is facing down, just from the way your hips and your pelvis are aligned, that means you're going to have hip pain soon.
