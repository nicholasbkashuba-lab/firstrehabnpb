Service

Home watch

Scheduled inspections of your empty home, run to the same protocol every time, documented with photos you actually receive.

What it is, and what it is not

Home watch is a scheduled visual inspection of a vacant or seasonally occupied home. It is not house sitting, and it is not a security patrol. It is early detection — somebody inside the property, on a schedule, looking at the specific things that fail in Florida houses, so that a small problem never becomes a five figure claim.

Almost nothing on our list is visible from the street. A supply line weeping behind a washing machine, a condensate drain backing up into a ceiling, humidity climbing because a breaker tripped, P-traps drying out and letting sewer gas in — all of it needs somebody inside, looking at particular things, in a particular order.

The five systems

Every visit runs the same sweep. The technician clears each line in the field, attaches a photo and a note to anything that is not a clean pass, and the report is generated from those entries rather than written up afterwards from memory.

System 01

Security

  • Doors & locks
  • Windows & sliders
  • Garage & overhead
  • Signs of forced entry
  • Alarm & camera status
System 02

Plumbing

  • Visible leaks
  • Water pressure
  • Water heater
  • Drains & traps
  • Toilets flushed, faucets run
System 03

HVAC

  • Cooling operation
  • Thermostat setpoint
  • Air filter condition
  • Interior humidity reading
  • Condensate line
System 04

Exterior

  • Roof line (visual)
  • Pool & spa
  • Landscaping
  • Irrigation
  • Exterior surfaces
System 05

Storm Readiness

  • Shutters & panels
  • Outdoor furniture secured
  • Storm supplies on hand
  • Drains & gutters clear
  • Generator & fuel

Each line is marked OK, Watch or Issue, and anything flagged carries its own photo and note in the report. See a full report →

How often

Most Florida carriers and home watch standards call for inspections every 7 to 14 days. Weekly is the most common choice for homeowners who leave for the summer, because humidity, power interruptions and plumbing failures move fast in this climate. A supply line that lets go on day two of a three month absence is a very different repair than one caught on day seven.

Many Florida policies also limit or exclude water damage coverage in a home left unoccupied beyond a set number of days unless it is being checked on a documented schedule. Ask your carrier what interval your policy requires and we will build the schedule around it. We are not insurance advisors, but we will happily send your agent a sample report.

Who actually walks your property

A HomeCrew technician who signs in as themselves. Their name is on the report, so every visit is attributable to a person rather than to a company. We are firefighter owned, fully insured and bonded, and proof of coverage is available before you sign anything.
