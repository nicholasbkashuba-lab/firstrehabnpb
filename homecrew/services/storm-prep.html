Service

Storm prep & response

Shutters up before the storm. A documented inspection within 24 to 48 hours after it. Neither one requires you to fly back.

Before the storm

When a storm is named and the track puts us in the cone, we work through the properties on our list. Shutters and panels go up, outdoor furniture, planters and grills come in or get secured, drains and gutters get cleared so water has somewhere to go, and we confirm generator fuel and storm supplies.

This is the part that cannot be improvised. Shutters that worked three seasons ago are not the same as shutters that work today — tracks corrode, panels go missing, wing nuts seize. Because storm readiness is one of the five systems on every routine visit, we find the seized track in February rather than on the day the cone appears.

There is a hard limit on this, and we would rather say it plainly: once conditions become dangerous, nobody goes out. Our crews are firefighters. They know exactly where that line is and they do not cross it for a shutter.

After the storm

Once conditions are safe we perform a post-storm inspection, typically within 24 to 48 hours, and send a photo report covering the roof line, windows, doors, pool cage, screens and any sign of water intrusion.

That report is what your insurer will want to see, and it is worth far more taken immediately than reconstructed weeks later. If repairs are needed we coordinate vendors and stay on site, so you are not trying to manage a roofer from another state.

What we cannot do

We are not a roofing company and we do not perform structural repairs. What we do is get eyes on the property fast, document what we find properly, bring in the right trades and be physically present while they work. For most owners the value is not the labour — it is not having to get on a plane.
